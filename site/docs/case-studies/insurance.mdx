## The business

A property and casualty insurer sells auto and home policies. Underwriting, finance and claims all need
analytics.

## The three fact types together

| Fact | Type | Grain | Facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fct_policy_transactions` | Transaction | policy coverage transaction (new, endorse, renew, cancel) | premium change amount |
| `fct_premium_monthly` | Periodic snapshot | policy coverage × month | written premium, earned premium (additive per month), in-force flag |
| `fct_claim_transactions` | Transaction | claim transaction (reserve, payment, recovery) | amount by transaction type |
| `fct_claim_pipeline` | Accumulating snapshot | claim | first notice, assigned, inspected, reserved, settled, closed dates; lags; totals to date |

## Techniques this industry forces

- **Heterogeneous products** (auto vs home coverages have different attributes: VIN, model year vs construction
  type, roof age). Use **supertype/subtype**: a core coverage and covered-item dimension, plus auto- and
  home-specific subtype dimensions and facts.
- **Policyholder mini-dimension** for volatile rating attributes (credit band, claims-history band, risk score
  band).
- **Timespan tracking:** coverage is in force over an interval. A policy-coverage fact row with
  `effective_date` / `expiration_date` answers "in force as of any date" with a range predicate.
- **Role-playing date and party dimensions:** transaction date, effective date; policyholder vs claimant vs
  third party.

```sql
-- Policies in force on a date, via timespan tracking
select count(distinct policy_key)
from fct_policy_coverage_timespans
where '2026-06-30'::date >= coverage_effective_date
  and '2026-06-30'::date <  coverage_expiration_date;
```

## Exercise

<details>
<summary>Actuaries want <strong>loss ratio</strong> (incurred losses ÷ earned premium) by line of business and accident year. Which tables, and how?</summary>

Earned premium from `fct_premium_monthly` (sum by line of business and year). Incurred losses from
`fct_claim_transactions` (payments + reserve changes, by line of business and the claim's **accident date** year,
a role-playing date). Aggregate each separately to line × year (**drill-across**), then divide. Never store the
ratio in a fact.
</details>
