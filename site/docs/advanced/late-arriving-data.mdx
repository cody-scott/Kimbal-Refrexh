# Late-arriving data

<KimballRef chapters={[19]} techniques={['Late Arriving Facts', 'Late Arriving Dimensions']}>
Kimball's "late arriving data handler" ETL subsystem (Ch. 19) covers both cases.
</KimballRef>

"Late" means out of order relative to what's already in the warehouse.

## Late-arriving facts

A fact arrives days or weeks after the event: a store's POS upload fails and catches up next week, or a
vendor sends last month's invoices.

Two things must go right:

1. **Dimension lookups use the event time**, not the load time. A sale from March must get the March product
   version, the March customer address, the March cashier role. The point-in-time join handles this
   automatically, because it uses `s.transaction_ts`:

   ```sql
   left join {{ ref('dim_customer') }} as c
       on  c.customer_id = s.customer_id
       and s.transaction_ts >= c.valid_from
       and s.transaction_ts <  c.valid_to
   ```

2. **The incremental model must pick it up.** A filter like `where transaction_ts > (select max(transaction_ts) from {{ this }})`
   misses rows dated before the high-water mark. Options:
   - a **lookback window** on the event date (the lab uses 3 days): simple, and catches most lateness;
   - filter on a **load timestamp** (`_loaded_at`) from the source instead of the business date. This is the most
     robust option, because a row that arrives late has a *new* load timestamp even though its event date is old;
   - periodic **full refreshes** of recent partitions for anything later still.

   With `merge` on the grain, reprocessing is idempotent.

Downstream, **periodic snapshots and aggregates for the affected periods must be rebuilt**. In dbt that happens
if they're downstream in the same run and either full-rebuilt or incremental with a matching lookback.

## Late-arriving dimension members

A fact references a dimension member that **doesn't exist yet**. Covered in depth in
[Special members](/dimensions/special-members#late-arriving-dimensions-inferred-members). In short:
create an **inferred member** row from the natural key, flag it, attach the fact, and overwrite the row's
attributes when the real record arrives.

## Late-arriving dimension *changes*

The subtlest case: you learn today that a customer **moved on March 1**, but sales since March 1 were already
loaded pointing to the old version.

| Approach | Effect |
|---|---|
| Classic ETL | Insert the new version with `valid_from = March 1`, then **update the surrogate key on every fact since March 1**. Expensive and error-prone. |
| dbt with a change log + rebuilt dimension | The dimension rebuild creates the version effective March 1. Facts must **re-resolve their keys**: a full refresh, or a lookback window long enough to cover the backdated change. |
| Accept it | Treat backdated changes as effective from when you learned of them. Simpler, and sometimes the business prefers it. |

Because hashed surrogate keys are deterministic (natural key + `valid_from`), rebuilding a fact from source
always re-derives the correct keys. That's one of the biggest practical advantages of the dbt approach over
sequence-keyed ETL.
