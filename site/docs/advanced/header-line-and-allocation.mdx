# Header/line facts and allocation

<KimballRef chapters={[6, 15]} techniques={['Header/Line Fact Tables', 'Allocated Facts', 'Profit and Loss Fact Tables Using Allocations']}>
Order Management (Ch. 6) covers header/line patterns and allocation; E-commerce (Ch. 15) builds a full
profit-and-loss fact by allocating costs to the lowest grain.
</KimballRef>

Many transactions have a **header** (order, invoice, ticket) and **lines**. Some measurements belong to the
header: shipping charge, order-level discount, tax, freight. How do they get into a line-grain fact table?

## What not to do

<Tabs>
<TabItem value="repeat" label="✗ Repeat on every line">

| order | line | product | net amount | shipping |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70001 | 1 | Tent | 349.00 | **15.00** |
| 70001 | 2 | Stove | 79.00 | **15.00** |
| 70001 | 3 | Fuel | 7.50 | **15.00** |

`sum(shipping)` = 45.00, but the customer paid 15.00.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="first" label="✗ First line only">

| order | line | product | net amount | shipping |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70001 | 1 | Tent | 349.00 | **15.00** |
| 70001 | 2 | Stove | 79.00 | 0 |
| 70001 | 3 | Fuel | 7.50 | 0 |

Totals are right, but "shipping by product" credits tents with all of it.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="alloc" label="✓ Allocate">

| order | line | product | net amount | allocated shipping |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70001 | 1 | Tent | 349.00 | 12.02 |
| 70001 | 2 | Stove | 79.00 | 2.72 |
| 70001 | 3 | Fuel | 7.50 | 0.26 |

Sums to 15.00, and is spread in proportion to value.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

The two correct designs are:

1. **Allocate** header facts down to lines, using a business-agreed rule.
2. Keep a separate **header-grain fact table** for header facts (Kimball discusses both; many models do both).
   The lab's `fct_order_fulfillment` is order-grain.

## The lab's allocation

Allocation details that matter in real projects:

- **Allocation basis is a business decision**: by value (as here), by quantity, by weight (better for freight),
  or equally. Document it.
- **Rounding must tie out.** Rounding each share independently can leave the total a cent off. The lab gives the
  remainder to the last line, and a singular test proves every order ties
  (`tests/assert_order_lines_tie_to_headers.sql`). A unit test covers the 3-way split of $10.00 → 3.33 + 3.33 + 3.34:

<DbtFile path="models/marts/orders/_orders__models.yml" lines={[30, 47]} title="_orders__models.yml (unit test)" />

- **Zero-value orders** (all lines free): guard the division with `nullif(..., 0)` and decide a fallback, such as
  equal split.

## Profit & loss facts

Taken to its conclusion, allocation lets you build a **P&L fact** at the atomic grain: revenue, then cost of goods,
allocated shipping, allocated payment fees, allocated marketing cost and so on down to **net profit per order line**.
Kimball notes this is among the most powerful and politically difficult tables to build, because every
allocation rule is contested. Store each allocated component as its own fact, so users can see (and argue
about) each one.
