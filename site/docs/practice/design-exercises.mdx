# Design exercises

For each scenario: **(1)** name the business process, **(2)** declare the grain, **(3)** list dimensions (with roles
and SCD types for key attributes), **(4)** list facts with additivity. Then sketch the star.

---

## 1. Gym memberships and check-ins

*A chain of climbing gyms sells monthly memberships and day passes. Members scan a card at check-in. Management wants
utilization by gym and hour, membership churn, and revenue.*

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

At least three fact tables:

| Fact | Type | Grain | Facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Check-ins | Transaction (factless + counter) | one row per check-in scan | check-in count |
| Membership monthly snapshot | Periodic snapshot | member × month | active flag, monthly dues (additive), tenure months |
| Membership lifecycle | Accumulating snapshot | membership | signup, first visit, freeze, cancel dates; lags |

Dimensions: date, time of day (hour bands like "After-work rush"), gym (with region), member (Type 2 on home
gym and membership tier; mini-dimension for age band and visit-frequency band), membership plan, pass type (junk:
day pass / guest / member). Degenerate: scan id.

Utilization = check-ins by gym × hour ÷ capacity (capacity from `dim_gym` or a capacity fact; drill-across).
Churn = cancellations in month ÷ active members at the start of the month (from the snapshot).
</details>

## 2. Ride-share trips

*A ride-share company needs trip revenue, driver earnings, surge impact and rider ratings.*

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

- **Process:** completed trips. **Grain:** one row per trip.
- **Dimensions:** request date/time, pickup date/time, dropoff date/time (role-playing date and time, local + UTC);
  pickup zone and dropoff zone (role-playing geography); rider; driver (Type 2 on vehicle and rating band);
  vehicle class; payment method; trip profile junk (surge tier, shared vs solo, scheduled vs on-demand);
  promotion. Degenerate: trip id.
- **Facts:** fare, surge amount, tips, tolls, platform fee, driver payout (additive); distance and duration
  (additive); rider rating (**non-additive**: average it; or store rating × 1 and a rating count).
- Also an **accumulating snapshot** of trip lifecycle (requested → accepted → arrived → started → completed)
  with lags, to analyze wait times and cancellations, which never become completed trips.
</details>

## 3. Software subscriptions (SaaS)

*A B2B SaaS company needs MRR, expansion and contraction, churn, and feature usage by account.*

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

| Fact | Type | Grain |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription MRR snapshot | Periodic snapshot | account × product × month |
| Subscription changes | Transaction | change event (new, upgrade, downgrade, cancel) with MRR delta |
| Feature usage daily | Periodic snapshot (or aggregate of events) | account × feature × day |

- MRR is **semi-additive** across months; **MRR deltas** (from the change fact) are additive and explain movement:
  starting MRR + new + expansion − contraction − churn = ending MRR. A consolidated "MRR bridge" fact per
  account × month holds those components.
- Account dimension: Type 2 on segment, industry and owner (for "as was" attribution); durable key for
  "as is" (Type 7).
- Feature dimension with a feature-area hierarchy; a **bridge** if a usage event can touch multiple features.
</details>

## 4. Warehouse shipments with multiple units of measure

*A distributor ships in pallets, cases and eaches. Sales thinks in cases, finance in dollars, the warehouse in pallets.*

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Fact grain: one row per shipment line. Store quantity in **one standard unit** (eaches) plus extended amounts;
put conversion factors (eaches per case, cases per pallet) on the **product dimension**, or on the fact when
packaging varies by shipment. Optionally carry `quantity_cases` and `quantity_pallets` facts if the factors vary
per shipment and users need fast access, but derive them all from one standard quantity in the same model so they
can't disagree.
</details>

## 5. Retail returns at Cascade Outfitters

*Returns can happen in any store or by mail, for store or web purchases. Merchandising wants return rates by product,
reason and original channel.*

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

This is the [capstone](./capstone). Key decisions:

- **Grain:** one row per returned product line.
- **Dimensions:** return date, *original purchase date* (role), product (as of the **original sale**, or as of the return? Decide
  and document; usually the original sale for rate calculations), return store (conformed `dim_store`, the Web
  Store for mail returns), original store/channel (role-playing `dim_store`), customer, return reason (small
  dimension with a reason group hierarchy), return condition (junk: resellable, opened, damaged).
- **Degenerates:** return id, original transaction id / order number (lets you join back to the original sale).
- **Facts:** returned quantity, refund amount, restocking fee (additive).
- **Return rate** = returned units ÷ sold units by product × month: a drill-across, never a stored ratio.
</details>
